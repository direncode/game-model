# Latent Ocean — IP Portfolio

This folder contains the inventor's IP-defense package for the
Latent Ocean stack, structured around a **two-phase IP strategy**:

**Phase 1 (today, founder-controlled, $0):** Bitcoin-anchored
cryptographic conception priority on the three crown-jewel
inventions, with full provisional-patent specifications drafted
and ready for filing at any time.

**Phase 2 (post-G42-strategic-stake, institution-funded,
$3–5M over 5 years):** Standard subsidiary-grade prosecution
program covering 80–130 patents across the inventive surface
area, including PCT and UAE national-phase filings, executed by
G42's retained IP counsel.

The current state is **Phase 1 fully activated, Phase 2 deferred
until strategic stake closes.**

---

## Why this two-phase strategy is the right founder posture

A solo founder filing 3 provisionals at micro-entity rates is
the *minimum-viable IP defense.* It is also a one-way commitment:
every USPTO-filed claim language locks in a specific position
that the acquirer's IP counsel may want to redraft, every PCT
filing burns the international filing window, and every $225
spent is $225 not redirected to actual product or outreach.

The alternative — used by sophisticated deep-tech founders
preparing for strategic-stake or acquisition — is to:

1. **Establish conception priority cryptographically** via
   Bitcoin-anchored OpenTimestamps proofs, which are
   court-admissible evidence of the invention's existence as of
   a specific date, without committing to USPTO claim language
2. **Maintain implementation as trade secret** in a private
   inventor-controlled repository under DTSA federal protection
3. **Draft full provisional specifications** so they are
   USPTO-filing-ready at any moment the strategic situation
   demands
4. **Defer USPTO prosecution to the acquirer's IP team**, which
   will (a) redraft to their own claim standards anyway, (b)
   coordinate with international filing strategy across the
   acquirer's jurisdictions of interest, and (c) prosecute under
   their retained counsel's relationships with USPTO examiners

This posture preserves all options, costs $0, signals founder
sophistication on IP economics, and matches what an acquirer's
M&A team actually wants to underwrite.
