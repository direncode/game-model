PHYSICS REGIME: RADIATIVE WIRELESS (detector/amplifier for
                Hertzian-wave receiving systems)
================================================================
This is the foundational Audion patent — the three-electrode
vacuum tube used as an amplifier / detector for feeble currents
produced by an antenna in a wireless-telegraph receiver. The
Audion itself is a device, but its intended application context
is receiving radiated Hertzian waves. The patent explicitly
shows an embodiment where the local circuit is the receiving
circuit of a wireless-telegraph system including an antenna V
and an oscillation detector T.

================================================================
INVENTOR DECLARATION (paraphrased from patent preamble)
================================================================

Lee de Forest, a citizen of the United States and a resident of
New York, declares the invention of a new and useful device for
amplifying feeble electrical currents.

================================================================
CORE APPARATUS
================================================================

"A represents an evacuated vessel inclosing a sensitive
conducting gaseous medium maintained in a condition of
molecular activity."

Three electrodes are sealed within the vessel:
- A heated filament E (of platinum, tantalum, carbon, or other
  suitable material), heated and preferably maintained
  incandescent by a battery B.
- Two non-heated plate electrodes D, positioned near the
  filament, unequally spaced in some claims.

The patent describes the residual gas inside the vessel as the
conducting medium whose ionization state is perturbed by the
incoming signal.

================================================================
SIGNAL MODULATION MECHANISMS
================================================================

De Forest describes several mechanisms by which a feeble input
signal modulates current in the local circuit:

1. Magnetic field interaction:
   An incoming signal's magnetic field can "react upon the
   field set up by the magnet N-S, thereby causing a slight
   variation in the separation between the electrodes D E."

2. Electrostatic effects:
   "The increase in electrostatic attraction between D E will
   cause E to recede from D, and thereby alter the current in
   the local circuit."

3. Ion motion control:
   Signals could "vary the motions of the ions around the
   filament."

Any of these mechanisms allow a small input current or field
variation to produce a relatively large change in the plate-
filament current through the ionized gas — the amplifying
action.

================================================================
WIRELESS APPLICATION (EMBODIMENT OF FIG. 2)
================================================================

"In Fig. 2 I have shown the line as constituting the local
circuit of a wireless-telegraph receiving system including the
battery B and oscillation-detector T, the latter being
connected in series with an antenna V."

So the Audion is explicitly positioned as part of a receiving
set for radiated Hertzian waves: an elevated antenna V feeds
the oscillation detector T, and the Audion amplifies the
resulting feeble current in the local circuit.

================================================================
REPRESENTATIVE CLAIMS
================================================================

Claim 1 (paraphrased):
Apparatus comprising an evacuated vessel inclosing a gaseous
medium maintained in a condition of molecular activity, two
electrodes sealed within said vessel, a local receiving
circuit associated with said electrodes, and means whereby the
separation of said electrodes may be varied by the currents to
be amplified.

Claim 6 (paraphrased):
A heated electrode and two non-heated electrodes sealed within
said vessel, the non-heated electrodes being unequally spaced
with respect to said heated electrode.

Additional claims cover combinations of the evacuated vessel,
filament, plate electrodes, and local amplifier/detector
circuits for telegraph, wireless telegraphy, and telemicrophone
(audio) transmission applications.

================================================================
APPLICATION AREAS (listed by de Forest)
================================================================

- Telegraph systems.
- Wireless telegraphy (receiving Hertzian waves).
- Telemicrophone (audio) transmission.

================================================================
NOTES ON PHYSICS (for structural discrimination experiment)
================================================================

The Audion is the canonical receive-side amplifier for
radiative wireless. Its context-of-use in this patent
(Figure 2) is an antenna-fed wireless-telegraph receiver
detecting radiated electromagnetic waves. There is no notion
of ground-return conduction, surface waves along the Earth,
or a Tesla-style Earth-resonant system. This patent should
classify as RADIATIVE / HERTZIAN-wave receiving apparatus —
a strong negative control for a structural discriminator
attempting to distinguish surface-wave Tesla-type systems
from radiative Hertz-type systems in the same time period.
